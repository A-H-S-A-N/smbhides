The maker

Forty days in the pit. Eleven hours at the bench.

Our hides are tanned slowly in oak and chestnut, then cut by hand and sewn with two needles on a single waxed thread. If a seam fails, it is the leather that gives way, never the stitch.

The first time someone's bag comes back after ten years, darker and softer and entirely theirs, that is when I know it was made right.Marta Bellandi, master cutter, third generation

Ethical footprint

What we hold ourselves to

Traceable to the farm

Every hide carries a lot number from the abattoir to the cutting table. Ask us for yours and we will send the chain of custody.

Slow, plant-based tanning

Most of our leather is tanned in chestnut, mimosa or oak bark. It takes weeks longer than chrome, and it ages better.

Built to be repaired

Seams, hardware and edge paint can all be renewed in our workshop for life. The best bag is the one you never replace.

Leather care

Look after it once a season.

Good leather asks for very little. Clean it, feed it, shield it from weather, and store it with room to breathe.

i. Clean

Brush, then wipe

A horsehair brush lifts dust from the grain. Use a barely damp cloth for marks, never soap on suede.

ii. Condition

Feed the hide

Work a thin layer of balm or oil in small circles. Let it rest overnight before buffing.

iii. Protect

Shield from weather

Wax or spray adds water resistance. Re-apply when beads of water stop forming.

iv. Store

Let it breathe

Stuff lightly with paper, keep in a cotton dust bag, away from radiators and direct sun.
